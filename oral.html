Texte complet

1

Ouverture

Good morning everyone. This file is top secret. Today, our team presents Operation Recorder-451: how we protect a very special archive, hidden in Zone 67.

2

L'équipe

We are five agents, each with a code name: COMMAND, FORGE, SENTINEL, CYPHER and RELAY. Each of us presents one part: the mission, the server, the security and the code.

3

COMMANDE

I am COMMAND, the team leader. My job today is to give you the mission, the threats and the big picture.

4

L'archiviste

Deep under Zone 67 lives Recorder 451. He is an alien archivist. He never speaks, but he keeps the memory of an entire civilisation: its history, its technology, and the truth about the Greys.

5

Les Gris

And here is the problem. The Greys, his enemies, are already inside the government. They look like normal civil servants, so nobody knows who is who.

6

La main

If they steal the archive, or if they destroy it, the truth is lost forever. That is why the Ministry of Defence called us.

7

Trois mots

Our mission fits in three words. Confidentiality: only the right people can read the archive. Integrity: nobody can change it or delete it without being seen. Availability: even after a fire or an attack, the archive comes back. In security, we call this the CIA triad.

8

Menaces

We face four threats. Traitors inside. Fake audits, people who pretend to be official inspectors. Destruction, by a breakdown or by ransomware. And spying on our network. So our rule is simple: we trust nobody by default, not even our colleagues. This is called zero trust.

9

Vue d'ensemble · 6 clics

Let me show you the big picture. Think of Zone 67 as a fortress. ▶ clic Everything from outside goes through one guard post: the firewall. By default, it says no. ▶ clic Inside, there are five separate buildings, each with its own badge door. We call them VLANs. ▶ clic The archive has its own vault. ▶ clic If a computer is infected in the users' building, it tries to reach the vault… and the firewall blocks it. ▶ clic Admins who work from home use a private tunnel, and come in through one escorted door. ▶ clic And every night, copies of the archive leave the site.

Conclusion

Thank you, RELAY. To sum up: a strong server, walls one behind the other, backups that nobody can destroy, and secure code. With all this, the archive stays available, intact, and out of the Greys' reach. Recorder 451 is fiction, but the danger is real: hospitals, cities and companies lose their data to ransomware every week. These rules protect them too. As I always say: watching is not enough. You must act.

Sources

Here are our five sources, all in English: CISA, Veeam, ANSSI, NIST and OWASP. Thank you for listening. Do you have any questions?

10

FORGE

Thank you, COMMAND. I am FORGE, the DevOps of the team. I built the server and the network. And at the end, I will come back to show you our recovery plan.

11

Le serveur · 5 clics

Let's look at the server, from the bottom to the top. At the bottom, there is one real machine. Almost everything is doubled: the disks, the network cables, the power supplies. ▶ clic On this machine, a software called Proxmox, the hypervisor, cuts it into virtual machines. It's like a building divided into rooms. ▶ clic One room for each job: accounts, files, the intranet, the alarms. If one room has a problem, the others keep working. ▶ clic The archive has its own locked room, in its own network zone, with an encrypted disk. ▶ clic And if a disk dies, its twin takes over. If the accounts server stops, its copy takes over.

12

Survivre à une panne

So our server is built to survive a failure. Four ideas. Mirrored disks: two disks write exactly the same data. Two power supplies, plus a big battery. Two account servers. And a minimal installation: we only install what we really need, because less software means fewer doors for attackers.

13

Cinq zones

Now the network. It is split into five zones. Zone 10 is for administration. Zone 20 is for the servers. Zone 30 is for the users' computers. Zone 40 is for the backups. And zone 50 is the vault: only the archive is inside, and it is never connected to the Internet. By default, nobody can go from one zone to another. The firewall decides.

→ SENTINELLE« But what if an admin needs to work from home? SENTINEL will explain. »
… SENTINELLE présente (slides 14 à 20) …
21

RPO / RTO · 5 clics

Thank you, SENTINEL. We have the copies. Now, how fast can we come back after a disaster? Our recovery plan uses two numbers. Look at this timeline. ▶ clic At nine o'clock, we have our last good backup. ▶ clic At eleven thirty, a ransomware hits the archive. ▶ clic All the work done since nine o'clock is lost. This gap is the RPO: how much data we accept to lose. ▶ clic We are back online at one thirty. This waiting time is the RTO: how long we accept to stop. ▶ clic For the archive, our target is never more than four hours, for both.

22

Le jour J

When disaster strikes, we follow four steps. Detect: an alert, or a user tells us. Contain: we isolate the infected zone first. Restore: the archive first, then the rest. Learn: we check, we write a report, and we improve. It's like a fire drill: everybody knows their role before the fire. And these two numbers, RPO and RTO, come from NIST, the American standards institute.

→ CYPHER« The infrastructure is safe. But the tools we use every day must be safe too. CYPHER codes them. »

Les grands points

  1. Présentation : I am FORGE, the DevOps → serveur + réseau, et je reviens pour le plan de reprise.
  2. Le serveur (5 clics) : one real machine, almost everything doubled → Proxmox, the hypervisor → one room for each job → the archive has its own locked room → a disk dies, its twin takes over.
  3. Survivre à une panne : mirrored disks, two power supplies + battery, two account servers, minimal installation → less software, fewer doors.
  4. Le réseau : five zones (10 admin, 20 servers, 30 users, 40 backups, 50 vault) → the firewall decides.
  5. Relais → SENTINEL (« work from home »).
  6. RPO / RTO (5 clics) : last good backup 9:00 → ransomware 11:30 → RPO = data we accept to lose → RTO = time we accept to stop → 4 hours max.
  7. Le jour J : detect, contain, restore, learn = a fire drill → source : NIST.
  8. Relais → CYPHER (« the tools we use every day »).

14

SENTINELLE

Thank you, FORGE. I am SENTINEL, the cyber security expert. I don't like to be seen… so my job is to see everything. I will talk about three things: remote access, our shield, and our backup protocol, Godkiller.

15

Quatre contrôles · 5 clics

An admin is at home, and he needs to fix a server in Zone 67. Direct access is forbidden. ▶ clic Checkpoint one: a private, encrypted tunnel, the VPN. To open it, he needs a digital ID card and a code on his phone. ▶ clic Checkpoint two: the firewall lets the tunnel reach only one door. ▶ clic Checkpoint three: this door is the bastion. It is an escorted entrance: it asks for another code, and it records everything. ▶ clic Checkpoint four: only then can the admin reach the server, and every action is traced. This idea comes from ANSSI, the French cyber agency: one controlled path for all admin work.

16

Défense en profondeur · 4 clics

Our security works like the walls of a fortress, one behind the other. This is called defence in depth. ▶ clic Outside: a locked room with a badge and a camera, then the firewall. ▶ clic Inside: separate zones, and machines with only what they need, always up to date. ▶ clic At the centre: personal accounts with a password and a phone code, and an encrypted archive. ▶ clic If an attacker breaks one wall, he meets the next one. And every wall sends an alert to our control room, the SIEM.

17

Quatre piliers

So our shield has four pillars. Strong login: a password plus a code, and each person only has the rights of their job. Encryption: stolen data is unreadable. Lockdown: everything is closed by default. And detection: the SIEM watches all the logs, day and night.

18

Règle 3-2-1-1-0

But what if everything is destroyed? This is why we have Godkiller, our backup protocol. Its rule is 3-2-1-1-0. Three copies of the data. On two types of media: disks and tape. One copy far away, on another site. One copy that nobody can erase. And zero errors, because we test our backups every month.

19

Pourquoi autant ?

Why so many copies? Because, according to Veeam, 93% of cyber attacks target the backups. Criminals destroy the copies first, so the victim has no choice: pay. And 75% of victims lose part of their backups. The American agency CISA says it clearly: keep offline, encrypted backups, and test them.

20

Architecture Godkiller · 5 clics

Here is how Godkiller works. Every night, the server sends an encrypted copy to a first vault, in another room. ▶ clic The encryption key is on paper, in another safe. Without it, a stolen backup is useless. ▶ clic A second vault, on another ministry site, comes to take the copies itself. Nobody in Zone 67 can log into it. ▶ clic Every Sunday, we write a special tape, called WORM: it is written once, and it can never be erased. It sleeps in a safe, with no cable. ▶ clic And to delete a backup, you need two people. One traitor alone can do nothing.

→ FORGE« We have the copies. Now, how fast can we come back after a disaster? FORGE, back to you. »

Les grands points

  1. Présentation : I am SENTINEL → I don't like to be seen, so I see everything → 3 sujets : accès à distance, Bouclier, Godkiller.
  2. Quatre contrôles (5 clics) : admin at home, no direct access → VPN + phone code → firewall, one door → bastion records everything → server, every action traced → source : ANSSI.
  3. Défense en profondeur (4 clics) : walls of a fortress → locked room + firewall → separate zones, updated machines → strong accounts, encrypted archive → every wall alerts the SIEM.
  4. Les 4 piliers : strong login, encryption, lockdown, detection.
  5. Règle 3-2-1-1-0 (l'idée expliquée du cours) : 3 copies, 2 media, 1 far away, 1 nobody can erase, 0 errors.
  6. Pourquoi : Veeam: 93% of attacks target backups, 75% lose some → CISA: offline, encrypted, tested.
  7. Godkiller (5 clics) : encrypted copy to vault 1 → key on paper → vault 2 comes to take copies → WORM tape, never erased → two people to delete.
  8. Relais → FORGE (« how fast can we come back? »).

23

CYPHER

Thank you, FORGE. I am CYPHER, the developer. The infrastructure is safe, but people read the archive through an application, and I code it. My job is to make this app secure from the inside.

24

L'appli · 5 clics

An archivist wants to read a document. Everything goes through my app. ▶ clic Gate one: the login. The password is never stored as it is. It is hashed: it becomes a fingerprint that nobody can turn back into the password. And there is a code on the phone. ▶ clic Gate two: the role. On every click, my code checks that this person has the right to see this document. ▶ clic Gate three: an attacker types SQL code in the search box, to trick the database. But we use prepared queries: his code stays plain text, and the attack fails. ▶ clic Finally, the documents are encrypted in the database, and every read goes into a log, sent to SENTINEL's control room.

25

Quatre règles

So, four rules for secure code. One: check access on the server, on every request. Two: no injection, thanks to prepared queries. Three: hashed passwords. Four: a second developer reads every change before production. Why is the first rule so important? According to OWASP, the global reference for web security, 94% of the apps they tested had a problem with access control. SENTINEL protects around the app; I protect it from the inside.

→ RELAY« My code is safe today. But what if a traitor changes it tomorrow? RELAY makes sure that never happens. »

Les grands points

  1. Présentation : I am CYPHER, the developer → I code the archive app → secure from the inside.
  2. Porte 1, la connexion : password hashed = a fingerprint + code on the phone.
  3. Porte 2, le rôle : on every click, my code checks the right.
  4. Porte 3, la requête préparée : SQL code in the search box → stays plain text → the attack fails.
  5. Au fond : documents encrypted, every read in a log → SENTINEL.
  6. Les 4 règles : check access on every request, no injection, hashed passwords, second developer reads every change.
  7. Source : OWASP, 94% of tested apps had an access control problem.
  8. Phrase clé : SENTINEL protects around the app; I protect it from the inside.
  9. Relais → RELAY (« what if a traitor changes it tomorrow? »).

26

RELAY

Thank you, CYPHER. I am RELAY, apprentice developer. I protect the road from the code to production. Because the most dangerous traitor is the one who can change the code.

27

Le chemin · 5 clics

Here is the road that every line of code follows. Step one: write. Every change is written on its own Git branch. Git keeps everything: who changed what, and when. ▶ clic Step two: review. A second developer reads every change. If a Grey hides a backdoor in the code, it is caught here. It's the two-person rule, for code. ▶ clic Step three: test. Automatic tests check that nothing is broken. ▶ clic Step four: scan. A scanner looks for dangerous code, and for outside libraries with known flaws. A trapped library is refused. ▶ clic Step five: release, but only if everything is green. And the passwords come from a secrets vault, never from the code.

28

Aucun Gris ne triche

So, no Grey can cheat in our code. Everything is traced. Four eyes see every change. Automatic checks run every time. And there is no secret in the code. A traitor's dream is a backdoor hidden in the code. Here, it never gets past the review.

→ COMMANDE« COMMAND, the floor is yours. »

Les grands points

  1. Présentation : I am RELAY, apprentice developer → the road from the code to production → the most dangerous traitor can change the code.
  2. Étape 1, écrire : own Git branch → who changed what, and when.
  3. Étape 2, relire : a second developer reads every change → a hidden backdoor is caught → two-person rule, for code.
  4. Étape 3, tester : automatic tests.
  5. Étape 4, scanner : dangerous code + libraries with known flaws → a trapped library is refused.
  6. Étape 5, déployer : only if everything is green + passwords from a secrets vault.
  7. Résumé : traced, four eyes, automatic checks, no secret in the code.
  8. Phrase clé : A traitor's dream is a backdoor. Here, it never gets past the review.
  9. Relais → COMMAND (« the floor is yours »).
