The mission

Recorder 451 knows the cosmic history, the alien technology and the truth about the Greys, who have quietly infiltrated the highest levels of government. If the Greys get his archives, or if the archives are destroyed, humanity loses its only independent record of what is really happening.

Vault location: sub-level 4, sector C. Confirmed Grey agents in the ministry: 3, including an auditor. Hover a black bar to declassify it.

Confidentiality

Out of the Greys' reach

Only authorised people can read the archive, and every access is logged.

Integrity

Intact

Nobody can change or wipe the archive without being detected, and we can prove it.

Availability

Always available

Even after a fire, a ransomware attack or sabotage, the archive comes back.

In plain words

These three goals are the CIA triad, the base of all cybersecurity: keep the secret, keep it correct, keep it reachable. Every technical choice on this page serves at least one of them.

The big picture

Everything goes through the firewall. Behind it, the core switch splits the site into five isolated zones (VLANs). The Recorder 451 archive has its own vault zone, and the backups have another one that nobody can reach from the users' side. [2]

Why five zones?

If a user's PC gets infected, the virus stays in the users' building. It cannot walk into the archive vault, the management room or the backup vault, because the firewall does not open those doors.

Say it

"Every packet crosses the firewall. The archive lives in its own vault, and each zone only talks to the zones it really needs."

The server: hardware, OS and services

One dedicated rack server runs a hypervisor, and the hypervisor runs seven virtual machines. Each VM does one job with minimal packages only, so a problem in one service does not take down the others.

In plain words

The server is one big building split into apartments (the VMs). The hypervisor is the building manager: it gives each apartment its own walls, water and electricity. RAID 1 means two disks write the same thing, so if one dies the other keeps going.

Say it

"One VM per role, with only the packages it needs: less software means fewer doors for the Greys."

Installation, step by step

  1. Rack and cableMount the dedicated server, plug both power supplies into the UPS, connect both 10 GbE links.
  2. Secure the firmwareUpdate BIOS and iDRAC, set a BIOS password, enable Secure Boot and TPM.
  3. Build the RAIDRAID 1 for the system, RAID 10 for VM data, with disk encryption.
  4. Install Proxmox VEStatic IP in VLAN 10, updates, admin account with MFA.
  5. Configure the networkLACP bond, VLAN-aware bridge, one VLAN tag per VM (VLAN 50 for ARCH01).
  6. Create the VMsFrom hardened templates, minimal packages only, deployed by Ansible.
  7. Deploy Active DirectoryCreate the zone67.lan domain, DNS and DHCP, then add DC02.
  8. Deploy the servicesArchive vault, file shares with rights per role, intranet, SIEM, bastion.
  9. Raise the ShieldApply ANSSI / CIS hardening (see the Shield section).
  10. Start GodkillerSchedule the backup jobs and run a first full restore test.

The Shield: defence in depth

No single protection is perfect, so we stack them. An attacker, or an insider working for the Greys, has to break every layer to reach the archive, and each layer raises an alarm. [3]

Strong authentication & RBAC

  • Personal accounts only, no shared "admin"
  • MFA on VPN, bastion, Proxmox and the archive
  • Roles: archive reader, archive admin, backup operator, auditor
  • Two-person rule: no single account can delete the archive
  • LAPS: a unique local admin password per machine

Encryption everywhere

  • At rest: LUKS on ARCH01, BitLocker on Windows VMs, encrypted backups
  • In transit: TLS 1.3, SSH, OpenVPN, IPsec
  • No clear-text protocols (no Telnet, FTP, HTTP)
  • Keys kept apart from the data they protect

Firewall & lockdown

  • Default deny on every VLAN
  • SSH and RDP only from the bastion
  • SSH keys only, no root login
  • fail2ban and account lockout after failed logins
  • USB storage blocked by GPO

Detection & patching

  • Wazuh agent on every VM, logs kept 1 year
  • Suricata IDS on the firewall
  • Alerts: new admin, archive access at night, mass deletion, backup failure
  • Monthly patching window
  • Monthly vulnerability scan (OpenVAS)
In plain words

The SIEM is the control room with all the camera screens. It collects every log from every machine and rings an alarm when something looks wrong, like someone opening the archive at 3 a.m. or deleting 10,000 files at once.

Say it

"We assume that one layer will fail one day. That is why we have six, and why every layer is watched."

The Godkiller protocol: automated backups

If the Greys destroy the archive, Godkiller brings it back. We follow the 3-2-1-1-0 rule, an extended version of 3-2-1 made for ransomware and sabotage. Every job runs by itself; humans only read the reports and test restores. [1] [2]

2types of mediadisks (PBS) and LTO tape
1copy off-sitePBS #2 at another ministry site
1copy immutableWORM tape in a safe, no network
0errorsverified every week, restore tested every month

Backup architecture

The Godkiller safeguards

Keys stored apart

Backups are encrypted on the host with AES-256. The key lives on paper in a sealed envelope, in a different safe, held by COMMAND. A stolen backup is unreadable.

Pull, not push

PBS #2 connects to PBS #1 and pulls the data. Nobody in Zone 67 has an account on PBS #2, so an infiltrated admin cannot wipe the off-site copy.

WORM tape

Write Once, Read Many: once written, the tape cannot be modified or erased, not even by an administrator.

Two-person rule

Deleting backups or changing the retention needs two approvals (COMMAND + SENTINEL). One traitor is not enough.

Monitoring & alerts

RELAY's dashboard shows every job in green or red. A failed or missing backup sends an email and a Wazuh alert within minutes.

Retention

PBS #1: 7 daily, 4 weekly, 6 monthly. PBS #2: 12 monthly. Tape: 12 monthly + 5 yearly, kept in the safe.

In plain words

Keep your house keys in three places: in your pocket, in a drawer at home, and at a friend's place in another city. Now lock one copy in a safe that nobody can open, not even you. Even a traitor with all your keys cannot destroy that one.

"Even if the Greys steal an admin account, they cannot erase the off-site copy or the tape. That is the Godkiller protocol."

Disaster recovery plan

Two numbers drive the whole plan. RPO is how much recent work we can afford to lose. RTO is how long the service can stay down. [4] [1]

When disaster strikes

  1. DetectAn alert from Wazuh, from RELAY's backup dashboard or a user report.
  2. AssessWhat is hit, since when, is it spreading, is it an insider?
  3. DeclareCOMMAND triggers the plan and informs the ministry.
  4. ContainIsolate the infected zone and lock suspect accounts before restoring anything.
  5. RestoreARCH01 and tier 1 first, then tier 2, using RELAY's restore scripts.
  6. Verify & reviewCheck the archive's integrity, write the report, improve the plan.
In plain words

A disaster recovery plan is a fire drill for computers. Everybody knows their role before the fire, so nobody panics during it. We rehearse it twice a year.

Say it

"For each scenario, we know what to do, who does it, and how long it takes."

Sources that justify our choices

Four reliable English sources: two official government guides, one U.S. standard and one industry survey. The small tags like [1] across the page show where each source supports a choice.

[1]

#StopRansomware Guide

★ Main source for the oral
Reference
CISA, FBI, NSA & MS-ISAC (U.S. government), updated September 2023. https://www.cisa.gov/stopransomware/ransomware-guide
Key idea
“Maintain offline, encrypted backups of critical data, and regularly test the availability and integrity of backups in a disaster recovery scenario.”
In our own words
Ransomware looks for backups it can reach and destroys them. So at least one copy must be unplugged, all copies must be encrypted, and a backup only counts if you have tested the restore.
Why it is relevant
Written jointly by U.S. cyber and intelligence agencies, so it is an official reference. It directly justifies our offline WORM tape, the AES-256 encryption and our monthly restore test.
Used in
Godkiller protocol · Disaster recovery
[2]

2023 Ransomware Trends Report

★ Main source for the oral
Reference
Veeam Software, press release of 23 May 2023, survey of 1,200 organisations hit by ransomware. https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20230523005553/en
Key idea
“Attackers almost always (93%+) target backups during cyber-attacks”, and 75% of victims lost at least some of their backup repositories.
In our own words
In almost every attack, the criminals go after the backups first, because without backups the victim is forced to pay. Three victims out of four lost part of their backups.
Why it is relevant
It gives real numbers that show why backups must be isolated, pulled by a separate server and kept immutable, exactly what Godkiller does. It is a vendor survey, so we use it for the trend and check it against the CISA guide.

Recommendations to secure administration of IT systems

Reference
ANSSI (French National Cybersecurity Agency), guide ANSSI-PA-022-EN, English version, 2018. https://cyber.gouv.fr/en/publications/recommendations-secure-administration-it-systems
Key idea
The guide sets “security objectives and the principles for developing a secure technical architecture for administration”.
In our own words
Administrator accounts are the keys to everything, so admin work must happen in a separate, protected zone, through controlled entry points, with strong authentication.
Why it is relevant
ANSSI is the French government agency that advises state administrations, including the Ministry of Defence, our client. It justifies our management VLAN, the bastion and MFA for admins, our first defence against infiltrated insiders.
Used in

SP 800-34 Rev. 1 — Contingency Planning Guide for Federal Information Systems

Reference
NIST (U.S. National Institute of Standards and Technology), 2010. https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/legacy/sp/nistspecialpublication800-34r1.pdf
Key idea
Defines the Recovery Time Objective (RTO) and the Recovery Point Objective (RPO) as the targets that drive a recovery plan.
In our own words
Before choosing backup tools, you decide how much data you can lose and how long you can be down. Those two numbers decide how often you back up and how fast you must restore.
Why it is relevant
It is the international reference for disaster recovery planning. Our tiers, backup frequencies and the RPO/RTO timeline follow its method.

Glossary

Useful for the audience, and for the questions at the end.

CIA triad
Confidentiality, Integrity, Availability: the three goals of security.
Zero trust
Never trust by default; check every user and every request.
RBAC
Role-Based Access Control: rights are given to roles, not to people.
Hypervisor
Software that runs several virtual machines on one physical server.
VM (virtual machine)
A complete computer simulated in software.
Active Directory (AD)
Microsoft's central directory of users, computers and permissions.
RAID
Several disks working as one, so a single disk failure loses nothing.
VLAN
A virtual network that isolates groups of machines on the same switch.
Firewall
The device that allows or blocks traffic between networks.
VPN
An encrypted tunnel across the internet.
MFA
Multi-factor authentication: a password plus a second proof, like a phone code.
Bastion
The single, monitored entry point for administrators.
SIEM
A system that collects all logs and raises security alerts.
Hardening
Removing everything unnecessary and locking down settings.
Full / incremental / differential
Copy everything / only changes since the last backup / all changes since the last full.
Immutable · WORM
A backup that cannot be changed or deleted once written (Write Once, Read Many).
PBS
Proxmox Backup Server: the backup software and storage.
Air gap
A copy with no network connection at all.
RPO
Recovery Point Objective: maximum data we accept to lose.
RTO
Recovery Time Objective: maximum time the service can be down.
